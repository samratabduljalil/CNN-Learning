Problem 1
Parameter explosion
Every pixel connects to every neuron. Millions of weights per layer — too slow to train.
Problem 2
No spatial awareness
Flattening destroys the 2D structure. Nearby pixels lose their relationship.
Problem 3
Not translation invariant
A cat in the corner vs center looks completely different. Must relearn for each position.

Local receptive fields
Each neuron looks at only a small patch of the image (e.g. 3×3 pixels) — not the whole thing. This mimics how biological vision works: cells in your retina respond to small local regions before combining into larger patterns.
Highlighted patch = one neuron's receptive field
⚖️
Weight sharing (shared filters)
One filter slides across the entire image. The same 9 weights detect the same feature everywhere — no relearning for each position. This is why CNNs are translation invariant and massively parameter-efficient.
Parameter comparison for 224×224 image:
Plain NN (1st layer, 1024 neurons)
~154 million parameters
CNN (32 filters of 3×3×3)
~864 parameters — 178,000× fewer
📦
Pooling (downsampling)
After convolution, pooling shrinks the feature map by taking the max or average of small regions. This reduces computation, adds robustness to small shifts, and lets deeper layers see larger context.
